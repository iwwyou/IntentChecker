## R1-1 — Reported Behavior Reconstruction

**Contract/function role.** `LiquidityBasedTWAP` is Vader's protocol-owned TWAP price oracle. It tracks a set of Uniswap-V2-style pairs (`usdvPairs`, each a foreign asset paired against USDV) and a Chainlink price feed per foreign asset (`oracles[asset]`), combining the two into a single USD-denominated price for USDV. `_calculateUSDVPrice` is the aggregation step: given per-pair liquidity weights and the accumulated `nativeTokenPriceAverage`/oracle data already stored in `twapData`, it is supposed to compute one scalar — "the price of 1 USDV in USD, at 18-decimal precision" (source's own in-line comment, L411: *"Accuracy of VADER & USDV is 18 decimals == 1 ether"*).

**Relevant locals/params (`_calculateUSDVPrice`, L385–413):**
- `liquidityWeights` (param, `uint256[] memory`) — per-pair weight, supplied by the caller (`syncUSDVPrice`/`getStaleUSDVPrice`).
- `totalUSDVLiquidityWeight` (param) — the weights' total, the common denominator.
- `totalUSD` (L389, accumulated L399–401) — running weighted sum of each pair's `foreignPrice` (Chainlink USD price of that pair's foreign asset).
- `totalUSDV` (L390, accumulated L403–408) — running weighted sum of each pair's `pairData.nativeTokenPriceAverage.mul(pairData.foreignUnit).decode144()` — a Uniswap-V2-TWAP-derived quantity expressing, in that pair's own foreign-asset unit convention (`foreignUnit = 10^(foreignAsset's decimals)`, set once in `_addUSDVPair`, L464–466), roughly "how much of foreign asset i one unit of USDV is currently trading for, per this pool's own TWAP."
- `foreignPrice` (L397) = `getChainlinkPrice(address(foreignAsset))` — Chainlink's raw, unscaled USD price for pair i's foreign asset (8-decimal, enforced by `require(oracle.decimals() == 8, ...)` at pair-registration time, `_addUSDVPair` L455).

**The buggy computation (loop body, L393–409, and the final division, L412):**
```solidity
for (uint256 i; i < totalPairs; ++i) {
    IERC20 foreignAsset = usdvPairs[i];
    ExchangePair storage pairData = twapData[address(foreignAsset)];

    uint256 foreignPrice = getChainlinkPrice(address(foreignAsset));

    totalUSD +=
        (foreignPrice * liquidityWeights[i]) /
        totalUSDVLiquidityWeight;

    totalUSDV +=
        (pairData
            .nativeTokenPriceAverage
            .mul(pairData.foreignUnit)
            .decode144() * liquidityWeights[i]) /
        totalUSDVLiquidityWeight;
}

// NOTE: Accuracy of VADER & USDV is 18 decimals == 1 ether
return (totalUSD * 1 ether) / totalUSDV;
```

**Reported erroneous behavior** (report's own words, quoted): *"It should be fairly clear that this is the incorrect calculation as all the terms in `totalUSDV` are in different units - you can't average the price of USDV in ETH with the price of USDV in BTC and get a meaningful result. ... It appears that the VADER team intended to calculate the price of USDV in terms of USD through a number of different paired assets and then average them at the end based on the liquidity in each pair but have started averaging too early."* No PoC numbers or recommended-fix code are given — the report is entirely qualitative (title + the paragraph above + "Review the algorithm used for calculating the prices of assets and ensure that it's calculating what you expect").

**Variable-value intent.** `totalUSDV` (and, downstream, `returnExpression`) must reflect a computation in which each pair's contribution has already been converted to a common (USD) unit **before** being combined across pairs — not a weighted sum of raw, pair-native-unit-denominated quantities whose implicit scale differs pair-to-pair (both because `foreignUnit_i` differs with each foreign asset's decimals, and, more fundamentally, because "USDV's price expressed in units of asset i" is not the same *kind* of quantity as "USDV's price expressed in units of asset j" unless i and j happen to have identical USD value per raw unit).

**Statement/line-level intent.** The function's loop is supposed to uphold: for each pair, first derive that pair's own independent estimate of "USDV's price in USD" (using both `nativeTokenPriceAverage`/`foreignUnit` *and* that pair's own `foreignPrice`), and only then combine (liquidity-weight-average) those already-USD-denominated per-pair estimates. As written, the loop instead accumulates two *separate* aggregates — `totalUSD` (a weighted average of `foreignPrice_i`, which *is* unit-consistent, since Chainlink prices for different assets are already all USD-denominated) and `totalUSDV` (a weighted average of `nativeTokenPriceAverage_i`-derived raw quantities, which is *not* unit-consistent across pairs with different foreign assets) — and only combines them via a single division at the very end, after the pair-specific unit information in `totalUSDV` has already been irreversibly lost to summation.

**Expected/intended behavior.** For each pair `i`: `USDVPriceInUSD_i` should be computed using that pair's own `foreignPrice_i` together with its own `nativeTokenPriceAverage_i`/`foreignUnit_i` (i.e., the exact expression the current code's *final*, single, un-per-pair combination step already applies — `(foreignPrice * 1e18) / decoded`, modulo the sibling case `70_H_05`'s separate missing-`1e10`-rescale defect — applied once **per pair**, not once at the very end over the pre-summed aggregates). The function's return value should then be the liquidity-weighted average of these per-pair `USDVPriceInUSD_i` values, not `(Σ w_i·foreignPrice_i / Σw_i) / (Σ w_i·decoded_i / Σw_i)`.

**Patch intent.** None available — unlike most cases in this dataset, the report gives no recommended-fix code, only the qualitative direction above ("intended to calculate the price of USDV in terms of USD through a number of different paired assets and then average them at the end"). This qualitative direction is itself sufficiently precise to derive the corrected per-pair-then-average structure above without needing any literal patch syntax (§2/§3's caution about mechanically transcribing patch syntax is moot here by construction, same as `70_H_05`).

**Note on H-05 (same function, different, independently-analyzed bug).** `web3bugs_70_H_05` ("Oracle returns an improperly scaled USDV/VADER price") is a separate finding in this *same* function: a missing `1e10` Chainlink-8-decimal-to-protocol-18-decimal rescaling factor, which manifests even at `totalPairs = 1` (no averaging involved at all). H-05's own analysis (`First Revision/phase_reviews/13_web3bugs_70_H_05/analysis.md`) explicitly scoped its target relation to the single-pair case specifically *to avoid* entangling itself with H-03's cross-pair issue, and flagged H-03 as "out-of-scope background" for that case. This case (H-03) is the mirror situation: it is **only** well-posed for `totalPairs ≥ 2` (with at least two foreign assets whose implied USD-per-unit values differ) — at `totalPairs = 1` there is nothing to average across, so H-03's defect cannot manifest at all, regardless of H-05's status. The two defects are logically independent and are treated that way here, per the task's instruction that `70_H_03`/`70_H_04`/`70_H_05` are analyzed by separate agents. Where the two defects' effects would otherwise be confounded in a concrete scenario (H-05's missing `1e10` factor is a uniform multiplicative constant applied to `totalUSD` immediately before the final division, independent of pair count or how `totalUSDV` was accumulated — see `70_H_05`'s own R1-1), the demonstration scenario below applies H-05's correction uniformly to both the "buggy" and "intended" computations, so the numbers being compared isolate H-03's own mechanism rather than mixing the two bugs' effects together.

**Concrete scenario / arithmetic (constructed — the report supplies no PoC numbers for this finding, unlike its H-05 sibling).** Two pairs: Pair A = a DAI-like 18-decimal stablecoin, trading at TWAP parity with USDV (`nativeTokenPriceAverage_A` decoding, via `.mul(foreignUnit_A).decode144()`, to exactly `1e18` — the same "ratio = 1, 18-decimal foreign asset" case `70_H_05`'s own report PoC uses); Pair B = an 8-decimal asset (e.g. WBTC-like) whose pool's own TWAP has, at the moment of this call, decoded to `2000` (an illustrative raw value — not derived from Uniswap's UQ112x112 internals, the same level of abstraction the report's own pseudocode uses when it writes `USDVPriceInForeign0`/`USDVPriceInForeign1` as opaque per-pair terms rather than deriving them from reserve ratios). Chainlink prices: `foreignPrice_A = 1e8` ($1, matching Pair A's TWAP), `foreignPrice_B = 5,000,000,000,000` (`5e12`, i.e. $50,000). Equal liquidity weights: `liquidityWeights[0] = liquidityWeights[1] = 1`, `totalUSDVLiquidityWeight = 2`.

- `totalUSD = (1e8·1 + 5e12·1) / 2 = 2,500,050,000,000`.
- `totalUSDV = (1e18·1 + 2000·1) / 2 = 500,000,000,000,001,000`.
- **Buggy, as literally computed by the current code** (includes H-05's own separate scaling defect, for reference): `return = (totalUSD · 1e18) / totalUSDV ≈ 5.0001×10¹²` — nonsensical either way (~$0.000005 for a token meant to be pegged near $1).
- **Buggy-H-03-only** (H-05's `1e10` rescale applied uniformly, isolating H-03): `(totalUSD · 1e10 · 1e18) / totalUSDV ≈ 5.0001×10²²` (≈ $50,001 in 18-decimal terms).
- **Intended** (per-pair USD conversion, then average): `USDVPriceInUSD_A = (foreignPrice_A · 1e10 · 1e18) / decoded_A = (1e8·1e10·1e18)/1e18 = 1e18` ($1 — Pair A alone is self-consistent, as expected). `USDVPriceInUSD_B = (foreignPrice_B · 1e10 · 1e18) / decoded_B = (5e12·1e10·1e18)/2000 = 2.5×10³⁷` (Pair B's own TWAP is, in this scenario, badly out of step with what its Chainlink price alone would imply — precisely the kind of temporary TWAP/oracle divergence the averaging step must remain sound under, since a Uniswap TWAP and an instantaneous Chainlink feed are never guaranteed to agree exactly). `intended = (1e18 + 2.5×10³⁷)/2 ≈ 1.25×10³⁷`.
- Buggy-H-03-only (`≈5.0001×10²²`) vs. intended (`≈1.25×10³⁷`) differ by roughly 15 orders of magnitude — a large, unambiguous discriminating gap, not a rounding artifact.

**A sharper, non-obvious point worth recording explicitly**: this construction also shows the bug does *not* manifest merely because pairs have different decimals/`foreignUnit`. Algebraically, if every pair's `decoded_i` happened to equal `foreignPrice_i · 1e10` exactly (i.e., every pair, evaluated in isolation, would independently agree that USDV ≈ $1), the buggy weighted-average-of-two-aggregates formula collapses to the *same* answer as the correct per-pair-then-average formula, regardless of how many pairs there are or how different their `foreignUnit` values are (both `totalUSD` and `totalUSDV` scale by the same constant across all pairs, so the constant cancels in the final division). The defect specifically requires the pairs to *disagree* about USDV's implied price (through TWAP staleness, temporary divergence, or manipulation) — a realistic condition for a live protocol, and exactly the condition an audit is concerned about, but a detail the report's own qualitative description ("you can't average price of USDV in ETH with price of USDV in BTC") does not spell out. This is recorded here because it directly shapes why the scenario above needed `decoded_B` chosen independently of `foreignPrice_B` rather than proportionally, and because it sharpens (without weakening) the scope of the intended relation in R1-2/R1-3 below.

**Bug-relevant intended numeric behavior**: for any `totalPairs ≥ 2` call, `_calculateUSDVPrice`'s return value must equal the liquidity-weighted average of each pair's *own*, independently-USD-converted price estimate (`Σᵢ (foreignPrice_i · 1e10 · 1e18 / decoded_i) · liquidityWeights[i] / totalUSDVLiquidityWeight`, where `decoded_i = pairData_i.nativeTokenPriceAverage.mul(pairData_i.foreignUnit).decode144()`) — not `(Σᵢ foreignPrice_i·w_i/W · 1e10 · 1e18) / (Σᵢ decoded_i·w_i/W)`, the aggregate-then-divide form the current code computes.

## R1-2 — Intent Abstraction

Distinguishing property (no patch syntax available to drop — none was given; derived directly from the report's own qualitative description and the function's existing per-pair variables): the function must combine each pair's contribution *after* converting it to a common (USD) unit, not sum raw, differently-unit-scaled per-pair quantities and divide once at the end.

**Intent-level orientation: Value-centered** — a constraint on the function's return value (`_calculateUSDVPrice` is `internal view`, no persistent state to transition; `totalUSD`/`totalUSDV` are ordinary locals).

## R1-3 — Select the least implementation-specific sufficient relation

1. **Directional (no explicit per-pair conversion)**: e.g. `returnExpression` bounded between `min(foreignPrice_i)` and `max(foreignPrice_i)` scaled appropriately. **Rejected — not meaningfully expressible or discriminating without per-pair values anyway** (see below; every candidate that references `foreignPrice_i` individually runs into the same blocker as the selected relation, so weakening the *comparator* buys nothing here — the blocker is about referencing the *operands*, not about how strict the comparison is).
2. **Aggregate-only inequality using just `totalUSD`/`totalUSDV`** (both freely in-scope, no call needed): e.g. `returnExpression` bounded by some function of `totalUSD`/`totalUSDV` alone. **Rejected — not sound.** `totalUSD` and `totalUSDV` are exactly the two buggy aggregates the report says were combined incorrectly; any relation built *only* from them, without reference to the individual `foreignPrice_i`/`decoded_i` terms that went into them, cannot distinguish "these aggregates were computed by first converting to USD" from "these aggregates were computed by summing raw units" — the information that would let a relation tell those two apart (which unit each per-pair term was in before summation) is exactly what is destroyed by the buggy accumulation itself. This is analogous to `34_H_01`'s R1-6 finding (a value destroyed by the very statement under test cannot be used to check that statement), except here it is the report's *entire reported mechanism* that is destroyed, not one parameter.
3. **Exact equality using per-pair values (SELECTED, notionally)**: `returnExpression == (Σᵢ (foreignPrice_i · 1e10 · 1e18 / decoded_i) · liquidityWeights[i]) / totalUSDVLiquidityWeight`, instantiated at a concrete `totalPairs = 2` (see the R1-6 quantification note below). This is the direct, faithful expression of R1-1/R1-2's intended behavior, ties the "convert first" property to the exact per-pair operands whose premature aggregation the report identifies as the defect, and is exact by nature — same reasoning `70_H_05` used for its own scaling constant: this is a "either the conversion happens per-pair or it doesn't" structural fact, not a threshold that admits a legitimate range of "partially correct" values (an implementation that converts only *some* pairs per-pair before averaging is still wrong, so a looser bound would not add real independence from the specific defect being targeted — see the required negation check below).

**Selected (notionally): Alternative 3.** R1-6 shows this cannot be constructed as a legal annotation using the current grammar/engine, for two independent reasons (a value-availability/call reason at R1-6/R1-7, and a related-but-distinct "no array quantifier" limitation noted separately) — the selection itself is not in question; its constructibility is.

**Required negation check (§3/§4).** Does the relation's negation fail to catch some alternative implementation that still retains the reported defect but produces it differently? Yes, worth recording per §3/§4's requirement: an implementation that pre-converts *some* pairs to USD before summing but not others (e.g., converts pair 0 correctly but still sums pair 1's raw `decoded_1` term directly) is a different buggy implementation than the one actually observed, and the selected relation (which checks the *fully* correct per-pair-then-average result) would still correctly flag it as violated — so this particular case does not actually expose a gap. A genuine, harder-to-catch alternative *does* exist, however: an implementation that converts every pair to USD individually but combines them with a **different**, still-plausible weighting scheme (e.g., weighting by each pair's *converted USD-denominated* liquidity rather than the raw `liquidityWeights[i]` the function already receives as a parameter) would also retain a "some cross-pair inconsistency" flavor of defect while producing a different numeric answer than the selected relation's exact `liquidityWeights[i]`-weighted average — the selected relation would not flag this variant as violated if it happened to coincide numerically, since the parameter `liquidityWeights[i]` is taken directly from the function's own inputs rather than independently re-derived. This is recorded for transparency (§3's required disclosure) — it does not change the selection, since `liquidityWeights[i]` genuinely is the weighting scheme this function's own signature and every other call site commit to, not an implementation-specific choice being smuggled in.

## R1-4 — During vs Post (natural choice, before the R1-6/R1-7 blocker)

**Natural choice: Post.** The relation concerns the function's return value — a function-exit property (README's During/Post criteria) — not an intermediate, statement-time value, and the property is inherently about how the *final*, fully-combined result relates to *all* pairs' contributions simultaneously, which only exists once the loop (L393–409) has finished. Not chosen merely because the report describes a function-level consequence (R1-4's explicit caution) — chosen because the quantity actually being constrained (the correctly-combined final price) has no meaningful "During," mid-loop reading.

**Explicit check against the `delta` loop-body exception (README §4/R1-7, mandatory per the task instruction), performed independently of `70_H_05`'s and `71_H_11`'s/`34_H_01`'s outcomes.** Is there a per-iteration `@During` alternative whose only viable attachment point is inside the `for` loop (L393–409), and would it hit the confirmed architectural exception? A per-iteration candidate was considered — e.g., checking, on iteration `i`, that `totalUSD`'s and `totalUSDV`'s increments are individually well-formed (`totalUSD == totalUSD(Before) + foreignPrice·liquidityWeights[i]/totalUSDVLiquidityWeight`) — but this is rejected on **content** grounds before the loop-body question even arises: the reported defect (H-03) is specifically about the *cross-pair combination*, not about whether any single iteration's own increment arithmetic is internally consistent (each individual increment, taken alone, *is* arithmetically exactly what the code intends — the bug is which quantity gets accumulated across iterations, not an error within one iteration). No single-point, single-iteration `@During` can express "this pair's contribution is unit-inconsistent with a *different* iteration's contribution," because the grammar's snapshot-qualified references (`(Before)`/`(After)`/`(Assign)`, README §4/R1-3) pin one variable's value at a different *time*, not a different array index or a different variable's value from another iteration — there is no construct that lets one loop pass reference another pass's locals. So this candidate fails on relation-form grounds, independent of, and prior to, the loop-body observation-point question. For completeness (matching the rigor of `34_H_01`'s and `70_H_05`'s explicit checks): **had** such a per-iteration relation somehow been expressible, its only viable attachment point would indeed be inside the `for` loop, and would therefore separately, independently hit the confirmed `delta` exception (`Interpreter/Engine.py`'s `fixpoint()`/`reinterpret_from()` never evaluating a `@During` inside a loop body) — but this is moot here, since the candidate was already rejected on content grounds first. **This case's actual blocker (below) is unrelated to delta.**

## R1-6 — Attempted construction of the target annotation (blocked — see below)

**Attachment point (would-be).** Immediately before the `return` statement (L412), i.e. the same point `70_H_05`'s Post annotation uses — `totalUSD`, `totalUSDV`, and `liquidityWeights` are all in scope there.

**The construction itself fails**, for two related but distinct reasons:

**(1) — `getChainlinkPrice()` cannot be referenced inside `intentValue`, and no known-bound rescue exists.** The corrected per-pair formula needs `foreignPrice_i` *for each individual pair* — not the pre-summed `totalUSD` (which only carries the *weighted-average* of `foreignPrice_i` across pairs, exactly the information-loss problem R1-3 alternative 2 identifies). `foreignPrice_i` is obtained **only** by calling `getChainlinkPrice(address(usdvPairs[i]))` (L397) — nothing stores a per-pair Chainlink price anywhere in contract state; it is fetched fresh on every invocation. `Parser/Solidity.g4`'s `arithFactor`/`varRef`/`subAccess` productions (checked directly, this session) allow only identifier chains, `.member` access, and `[index]` access inside `intentValue` — there is no function-call production anywhere in that grammar subtree, so `getChainlinkPrice(...)` cannot be written inside an annotation at all, regardless of scope. Per README's mandatory check (§4/R1-3): is there a known bound on `getChainlinkPrice`'s result that would let the relation avoid needing the call itself (the Nokon-style rescue)? `getChainlinkPrice` (L82–96) only enforces `answeredInRound >= roundID` (staleness) and `price > 0` (positivity) — no upper or lower numeric bound on the price itself is enforced anywhere in the contract. `price > 0` is far too weak to discriminate this defect (both the buggy and the intended computation are consistent with "some positive Chainlink price" for every input; the defect is about *how differently-priced pairs' contributions are combined*, which requires knowing the actual relative magnitudes, not merely their sign). **No rescue exists — this is squarely alpha**, not a case where the rescue check was skipped.

**(2) — A secondary, narrower finding worth recording: decoding `nativeTokenPriceAverage` itself is *not* independently blocked.** `pairData.nativeTokenPriceAverage.mul(pairData.foreignUnit).decode144()` (the FixedPoint library calls used in the *source*) would, if copied literally, also look like a call-inside-`intentValue` problem — but this one *does* have a rescue. `FixedPoint.sol` (`evaluation/RQ1/target_contracts_original/dependencies/FixedPoint.sol`, read this session) defines `uq112x112` as a bare one-field struct (`struct uq112x112 { uint224 _x; }`, L12–14) and implements `.mul()`/`.decode144()` (L38–61) as ordinary arithmetic on that raw field (`self._x >> RESOLUTION`, `self._x * y`, `RESOLUTION = 112`) — a plain right-shift-by-112 decode with no rounding/branching logic beyond an overflow `require`. Since the grammar's `subAccess` allows `.identifier` member access on structs, and `arithExpr` includes `<<`/`>>`/`>>>` as first-class operators (`Solidity.g4` L347), the *decoded* per-pair TWAP quantity **is** directly expressible without any call: `(twapData[address(usdvPairs[i])].nativeTokenPriceAverage._x * twapData[address(usdvPairs[i])].foreignUnit) >> 112` reproduces `.mul().decode144()`'s result using only member access, indexing, multiplication, and a bit-shift — all legal `arithFactor`/`arithTerm`/`arithExpr` forms. This means `decoded_i` (this case's `70_H_05`-style "already-decoded TWAP value") is **not** what blocks this case — only `foreignPrice_i` is. This is recorded explicitly because README's methodology requires trying the rescue before declaring inexpressible (§4/R1-3), and because it would be inaccurate to write off the whole relation as "full of calls" — only one of its two per-pair ingredients is actually alpha-blocked, but that one ingredient is load-bearing and has no rescue of its own.

**Secondary, independent limitation (not the primary blocker, recorded for completeness).** Even setting the `getChainlinkPrice` blocker aside entirely, the intended relation is naturally stated as a sum over an arbitrary number of pairs (`Σᵢ...` for `i` in `0..totalPairs-1`). Per README §4/R1-6, the grammar has no construct letting an annotation itself range over an array/mapping — any concrete annotation would necessarily have to fix a specific `totalPairs` (e.g. instantiate at `totalPairs = 2`, as the R1-1 scenario does), the same "quantified property instantiated" pattern used elsewhere in this dataset (`83_H_01`, `52_H_34`). This is a real, independent constraint on top of the `getChainlinkPrice` blocker — it does not, by itself, force Inexpressible (a concrete-`n` instantiation is a legitimate, sanctioned workaround), but it means that even in the counterfactual where alpha did not apply, this case's target annotation would still only ever certify the property for one fixed pair-count, not the fully general "for any number of pairs" claim the report's title implies. Noted here for transparency, not as a second independent blocker.

**Conclusion**: R1-6 cannot produce a fixed target annotation, at any legal program point, that both (a) is grammatically legal and (b) faithfully expresses the R1-2/R1-3 relation — the per-pair `foreignPrice_i` operand has no call-free in-scope reference, and no bound rescues it.

## R1-7 — Expressibility decision

- **Values referenceable at a legal point**: **Partially.** `totalUSD`, `totalUSDV`, `liquidityWeights[i]`, `totalUSDVLiquidityWeight` are ordinary in-scope values; the per-pair *decoded* TWAP quantity (`decoded_i`) is also referenceable without a call, via raw struct-field access and bit-shift (R1-6, point 2). The per-pair `foreignPrice_i`, however, has **no** in-scope reference of any kind except a live call to `getChainlinkPrice()`, which the grammar disallows inside `intentValue`, and no known bound on that call's result discriminates the defect (R1-6, point 1).
- **Arithmetic/logical relation representable**: Yes, in isolation — multiplication, division, member/index access, and bit-shift are all ordinary, well-supported `arithTerm`/`arithExpr`/`subAccess` forms; this is not the blocking factor.
- **Observation point supported**: Not reached — the value-availability blocker above is dispositive regardless of attachment point. (The explicit delta check, R1-4, confirms independently that this case's real defect is not a loop-body-observation-point problem at all — the per-iteration candidate that *would* have hit delta was already rejected on content grounds before the observation-point question arose.)

**Outcome: Expressible = NO.**

**Tag: alpha** — the relation needs a function call (`getChainlinkPrice`) inside `intentValue` to obtain a value (`foreignPrice_i`, per individual pair) with no in-scope non-call reference, and no known bound on that call's result (only `price > 0`, far too weak) rescues the gap, per the mandatory check (README §4/R1-3). This is the *only* independently-sufficient blocker — the decode-call concern (`.mul()/.decode144()`) that might naively also look like alpha turns out to have a working rescue (R1-6, point 2) and is **not** part of why this case is Inexpressible; recorded to show the rescue check was genuinely performed, not skipped, and did partially succeed. The loop-body `delta` exception (README §4/R1-7) does **not** apply here either — checked explicitly in R1-4 and found inapplicable, on different (content, not observation-point) grounds, before it was even relevant. Not tagged `alpha_and_delta`: delta specifically requires an otherwise-sound relation whose *only* problem is attachment location; here the only viable per-iteration alternative already fails on relation-form grounds, so delta was never actually in play, only checked and ruled out for completeness.

## §5 — Value/Algorithm and Usable/Unusable

- **Algorithm-level**: the relation would tie together several derived per-pair quantities (each pair's own Chainlink price and its own decoded TWAP rate) via a nontrivial two-step recombination (per-pair conversion, then weighted average) — not a raw threshold on a single input, and structurally different from what the current code computes (weighted-average, then a single conversion).
- **Unusable** — the value needed to represent the intended relation (`foreignPrice_i`, the per-pair Chainlink price at the time of the original call) is not referenceable in any form the intent model can use: it exists only as the result of a call the grammar disallows inside `intentValue`, and no bound substitutes for it. This is purely a representational-resources fact (§5), not a claim about whether a developer could have identified the mechanism.
